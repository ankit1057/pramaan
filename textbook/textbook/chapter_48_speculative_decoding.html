1. The Slow Master Chef vs The Speedy Apprentice

In a 3-star Michelin kitchen:
• The Master Chef (70B Target Model) has sublime culinary wisdom, but he moves deliberately slowly. He takes 15 seconds to prepare each dish.
• The Apprentice (1B Draft Model) is young and lightning fast: he can chop 5 onions in 1 second, but he occasionally makes an imperfect cut.

🍳 The Speculative Decoding Teamwork

Instead of the master chef doing all the slow chopping alone:
1. The apprentice rapidly chops 5 candidate onions in 1 second.
2. The master chef glances at all 5 chopped onions SIMULTANEOUSLY in a single 1-second parallel look!
3. The master evaluates:
  • Onion 1: Perfect! (Accepted).
  • Onion 2: Perfect! (Accepted).
  • Onion 3: Perfect! (Accepted).
  • Onion 4: Bad cut! (REJECTED).
4. The chef accepts the first 3 onions immediately. For Onion 4, he throws away the bad cut, makes one clean cut himself from his own recipe book, and they start the next batch!
$\implies$ The kitchen produces dishes $3\times$ faster, and every single dish served to customers is 100% MASTER CHEF QUALITY!

🧠 Permanent Memory Anchor #1
The D.R.A.F.T. Protocol
Draft fast, Review parallel, Accept ratio, Fallback To residual!
1. Small model drafts $K$ tokens.
2. Large model reviews all $K$ tokens in ONE parallel forward pass.
3. Accept token $x$ with probability $\alpha = \min\left(1, \frac{P_{\text{large}}(x)}{Q_{\text{small}}(x)}\right)$.
4. If rejected, sample replacement from residual distribution: $P'(x) = \frac{\max(0, P - Q)}{\sum \max(0, P - Q)}$.

2. The Mathematical Proof of Zero Degradation

Many engineers assume speculative decoding is a lossy heuristic or an approximation.
It is NOT! The output probability distribution is mathematically 100% IDENTICAL to the large model alone!

Theorem: Exact Distribution Parity
For any token $x$: $$\mathbb{P}(X = x) \equiv P(x)$$

The 3-Step Proof:

Token $x$ can be chosen in two mutually exclusive ways:
1. Proposed by draft model and accepted: $\mathbb{P}(\text{accepted}) = q(x) \times \min\left(1, \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right) = \min(q(x), p(x))$.
2. Rejected, and sampled from residual distribution $p'(x)$: $$\mathbb{P}(\text{rejected}) \times p'(x) = \left[\sum \max(0, p - q)\right] \times \frac{\max(0, p(x) - q(x))}{\sum \max(0, p - q)} = \max(0, p(x) - q(x))$$ Now add them together: $$\mathbb{P}(X = x) = \min(q(x), p(x)) + \max(0, p(x) - q(x))$$ Notice the fundamental algebraic identity for ANY two real numbers $a$ and $b$: $$\min(a, b) + \max(0, a - b) \equiv a$$ Here, letting $a = p(x)$ and $b = q(x)$: $$\mathbf{\mathbb{P}(X = x) \equiv p(x)} \quad \blacksquare$$

🧠 Permanent Memory Anchor #2
The P.A.R.I.T.Y. Identity
"Min plus Max-of-Diff equals Pure Target!"
$\min(Q, P) + \max(0, P - Q) = P$.
Whatever the small model gets right, we keep. Whatever it gets wrong, the residual distribution mathematically compensates for with zero distortion!
