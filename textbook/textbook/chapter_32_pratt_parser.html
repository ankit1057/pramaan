1. The Tug-of-War: Who Wins the Middle Number?

Look at this simple expression: $$3 + 4 \times 2$$ The number 4 is stuck in the middle.
To its left, the plus sign ($+$) wants to pull it into an addition: $(3 + 4) = 7$.
To its right, the multiplication sign ($\times$) wants to pull it into a multiplication: $(4 \times 2) = 8$.

🤼 The Operator Wrestling Match

Think of operators as wrestlers with different muscle mass (Binding Power):
• Addition ($+$) has a light muscle strength: Power = 10.
• Multiplication ($\times$) has heavy muscle strength: Power = 20.

When 4 is caught between $+$ and $\times$, Multiplication flexes its 20 power against Addition's 10 power.
$\implies$ Multiplication easily wins the tug-of-war!
4 is yanked to the right first: $4 \times 2 = 8$.
Then, the weakened $+$ operator gets to add the result: $3 + 8 = \mathbf{11}$.

🧠 Permanent Memory Anchor #1
The P.R.A.T.T. Principle
Power Ranks Attach Tokens Tightly!
Instead of writing separate grammar rules for every precedence level, give each operator an integer Binding Power.
A loop continues consuming tokens as long as the next operator's Binding Power is strictly greater than the current context's threshold!

2. The Two Faces of a Token: `nud` vs `led`

In Pratt's 1973 paper, he recognized that any symbol in a language can only be used in one of two contexts:

🎭 The Two Roles

1. `nud` (Null Denotation):
The token appears at the start of an expression with nothing to its left!
• Numbers: 42
• Identifiers: user_income
• Prefix operators: -x or !is_valid
• Grouping parentheses: (3 + 4)

2. `led` (Left Denotation):
The token appears between expressions, connecting to an existing value on its left!
• Infix operators: left + right, left * right, income >= 5000

🧠 Permanent Memory Anchor #2
N.U.D. vs L.E.D.
Nud = New tree root!
Led = Links to left!
• nud(): Takes NO arguments. Starts a fresh node.
• led(left): Takes the existing left subtree as its argument and wraps it into a binary node!

3. Dhwani Real-World Case: Frappe Mobile SDK PR #105

In Dhwani rural field apps, dynamic forms rely on depends_on expressions to hide or show questions: 

eval:doc.income > 5000 && doc.age >= 18 || doc.has_special_permit == 1
Originally, developers attempted to parse this using regular expression string replacement hacks.
When nested conditions or negative numbers were introduced, regex broke catastrophically!
In PR #105, Ankit designed a clean Pratt Parser Engine in Dart with binding powers:
• ||: Binding Power 10
• &&: Binding Power 20
• >, >=, ==: Binding Power 30
• +, -: Binding Power 40
• *, /: Binding Power 50
This guarantees mathematical evaluation without regex vulnerabilities!
